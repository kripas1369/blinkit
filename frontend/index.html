    How to use Blink

    Compare the same patch of sky across SPHEREx visits, then share the view by URL.

    1. Pick one of the official targets, or search a star or coordinates at the top.
    2. Set field of view and detector in the Sky map panel (the white square on the map).
    3. When frames are ready, flip through visits. What moves or fades is worth a closer look, but it is not a discovery.
    4. Use Share to copy a link with ra/dec (and FOV/detector) so others — or another website — can open the same view.
    5. Scroll to Six Eyes for deep stacks of all six detectors side by side, plus a coarse spectrum.

    The sky is never the same twice.

          Six Eyes

          Same sky, six detectors — each image is a deep stack of all visits for that array. Tap an eye for a large card view; use arrows to step D1→D6.

          Coarse spectrum

          What this shows: brightness near the target in each detector’s deep stack, plotted against that detector’s wavelength (short → long: D1…D6). A rising or falling shape means the source is brighter in some infrared bands than others — a quick colour fingerprint, not a full 102-channel science spectrum.

          Each point averages light inside one detector’s band (many LVF channels mixed). Good for comparing D1–D6 at a glance; use Lab 2 / IRSA for finer spectra. Fast movers can smear in the deep stacks.
